Hybrid system power and 30-minute power of electric motors

In the traditional Toyota Hybrid System (THS/HSD), the powers of the combustion engine and traction motor cannot be summed arithmetically, because THS is not a pure parallel, but a serial-parallel hybrid. When the gasoline engine and the electric motor work together, a significant part of the internal combustion engine power is spent on driving the MG1 generator, which together with the battery supplies the necessary energy for the MG2 traction motor.

Since 2003, Toyota has published the estimated values of total output of hybrid system (システム最高出力, Systemleistung etc.) for each specific model. Easy to see, in most cases it is approximately equal to the sum of the MG2 motor power and a half of the ICE maximum power, while for e4WD models the power of the rear electric motor is not taken into account.

In a number of cases, the company directly states that the hybrid system total power is equal to the sum of the ICE power and the maximum power supplied by the high-voltage battery. So the specs the electric motors themselves are not important for determining this value, therefore the declared system power of the all-wheel drive version is equal to or only slightly exceeds the system power of the front-wheel drive version.

For the new parallel type (DBHS/T-HEV), the power of the ICE and front electric motor is summed up (not arithmetically). For structurally simple parallel type of heavy SUVs (L4A0), the powers of the ICE and the auxiliary electric motor are added directly. For the series type inherited from Daihatsu (e-Smart Hybrid), the powers do not add up.
